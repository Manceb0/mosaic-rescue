## Targeted caption–methods reconciliation

1. **Cell identity:** Suijkerbuijk Fig. 5B is U2OS, not HeLa. Fig. 4D mixes U2OS rescue experiments with a HeLa depletion titration. These are not patient-cell error-rate measurements.
2. **Construct identity:** Suijkerbuijk's discussion says the published truncation construct contains only 715 amino acids of native BUBR1 sequence. Its historical name must not be treated as a simple domain boundary or as the exact case product. Cross-paper construct equivalence requires supplementary sequence confirmation.
3. **Expression matching:** Gama Braga main text states doxycycline titration to equivalent kinetochore levels and cites S4A. **S4A now inspected** (supplement, PDF supplied by the team): levels were experimentally matched: no significant difference was detected in kinetochore GFP-MYC-BUBR1/CENP-C between WT, the truncation, DKD and KD under the reported assay (at least 10 cells per condition, one representative of 3 experiments). This is not proof of equivalence. S5A–B likewise detect no significant difference in whole-cell GFP and MYC levels in the cells used for the checkpoint-exit (Fig. 4F) and alignment (Fig. 4G) assays. Limits: "n.s." is not an equivalence test, cell numbers are small, and per-cell statistics come from one representative experiment. Whole-cell matching alone cannot establish matched kinetochore occupancy; do not label a construct “physiological” merely because it matches an overexpressed WT comparator.
4. **Exit versus faithful division:** Fig. 4F uses nocodazole plus reversine. It measures forced checkpoint exit, not ordinary error-free mitosis. Fig. 4G/I measures misaligned kinetochores after STLC release into MG132. Neither replaces new errors per completed mitosis.
5. **Image quantification:** Methods divide the pole-to-pole area into five zones and count outside the central zone. This is a count per cell, unlike a binary aligned/misaligned-cell fraction; the papers' numbers cannot be directly pooled.
6. **Replication:** Many Gama Braga dot plots show individual cells from one representative experiment, although three experiments were performed. The statistical section uses unconventional technical/biological replicate terminology. Preserve the nesting of cells within experiments and report experiment-level uncertainty; do not turn cell counts into independent experimental replication. Raw-data reanalysis remains pending.
7. **Negative controls:** Fig. 6 argues against CENP-E being the explanation of the B56/exit phenotype under its conditions. Fig. 7D compares BUB1 states at maximal truncation expression; it does not establish a truncation dose-response, restoration to WT, or rescue of downstream layers. “Not significant” is not proof of equivalence.
8. **Reconciliation:** Matched-level defects and low detectable phospho-signal at high expression can coexist. The current evidence supports endpoint/context dependence; it does not identify overexpression as the sole explanation, exclude its contribution, or prove a pharmacological bypass.

| A6 | Compare case missense with WT at matched measured abundance | Expression-response curve and separate total amount, kinetochore occupancy, molecular function and distal outcomes | Matching total amount excludes localization defects; improvement proves a pure stability mechanism |

**Proposed design, not an executed protocol.** A5 tests WT versus exact truncation expressed independently of NMD; A6 tests WT versus case missense. For both, define expression bins spanning below-reference, near-endogenous-reference, matched-WT and higher expression, with actual measured abundances rather than induction labels. Near-reference and matched-WT may coincide and should not be double-counted. Calibrate the reference and freeze analysis thresholds before evaluating effects.

Score separately: total protein/turnover; kinetochore abundance; KARD phosphorylation; PP2A-B56 recruitment; CENP-E interaction and localization; checkpoint timing; alignment; **new errors per completed mitosis at matched proliferation**; and survival/propagation of unstable cells. Include WT, depletion/empty-vector controls and appropriate assay-specific controls; define batch/cell nesting and blinded quantification prospectively. Do not infer safety from a molecular endpoint. A5 cannot determine endogenous transcript availability; RNA remains a separate requirement.

If all measured layers recover, the conclusion is: **under the assayed conditions, the measured deficit is compatible with abundance being a dominant bottleneck**. If defects remain after matching both total and kinetochore amounts, a pure abundance explanation is insufficient in that assay. Partial recovery must name the layers and expression range. Neither outcome nominates a drug by itself.

## Project implications and pending work

- Observation: the same general domain question gives different answers across readouts. Interpretation: “rescue” must specify layer, expression and system. Another team could miss this by reading abstracts or collapsing total protein into functional protein.
- Counter-evidence: retained CENP-E binding in KD/DKD and Fig. 6 controls challenge a single linear CENP-E-to-B56 mechanism. High-expression phospho-signal is not a complete rescue result.
- Required modality: controlled protein/cell measurements; current bulk blood WGS cannot test A5/A6 or reveal successful patient lineages. No patient experiment is run here.
- Independent validation: exact-allele isogenic models followed, if feasible, by patient-derived cells with independent biological replicates and the safety readouts. Failure to improve new-error rate or increased propagation of unstable cells falsifies a safe-rescue interpretation.

## BUBR1–CENP-E block (added 2026-09-29; full text read, figures not yet inspected)

- Legal et al. 2020, J Cell Sci, PMID [32665320](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32665320/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.246025): the basic C-terminal helix of BUBR1 (last 20 residues) is necessary, not sufficient, for CENP-E binding. With a single-copy construct lacking it, expressed at levels similar to endogenous BUBR1: CENP-E recruitment fails after a short (5 min) nocodazole pulse, while after 2.5 h the authors describe levels as "much more similar" (not equivalent); more cells keep a few non-congressed chromosomes. The construct is BUBR1 1–1030, a specific ~20-residue deletion of the C-terminal helix: it is **not** the historical truncation and **not** the case truncation, so it tests the helix, not the case allele.
- Weber et al. 2024, Curr Biol, PMID [38354735](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38354735/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2024.01.042): a second, slower, BUBR1-independent route, mediated by a conserved CENP-E region, targets CENP-E to the **outer corona** (RZZ, Spindly, dynein); maximal recruitment to unattached kinetochores depends jointly on BUBR1 and the corona. Kinetochore-bound (BUBR1-route) and outer-corona CENP-E are different compartments and must not be merged into "CENP-E present".
- **Consequence.** "The truncation cannot bind CENP-E → CENP-E is absent → alignment defect" is not a valid inference. The right questions are how much CENP-E arrives, how fast, at which kinetochores and by which route. **Rescue can be time-dependent as well as endpoint-dependent**: a late snapshot can hide an early recruitment defect.
- For the case (prediction only): the exact truncation lacks the C-terminal helix required for the BUBR1–CENP-E interaction in Legal 2020, **predicting** impaired rapid CENP-E recruitment; this remains to be tested for the exact construct; the missense lies inside the pseudokinase domain about 26 Å from that helix in the AlphaFold model (descriptive, not a mechanism call; the helix itself has mean pLDDT about 65).

## A5/A6 readout hierarchy (revised before freeze)

abundance → kinetochore BUBR1 → KARD phosphorylation / PP2A-B56 → CENP-E binding → **CENP-E recruitment kinetics (early pulse vs prolonged arrest)** → attachment / K-fibres → checkpoint-silencing timing → alignment → new segregation errors per completed mitosis in unperturbed mitosis → safety (survival and propagation of unstable cells).

Each layer is scored separately, with expression-response curves and total and kinetochore levels reported apart, along three axes where technically feasible: **time** (early vs late), **compartment** (kinetochore-bound vs outer corona) and **function**. Two readout families, never merged: (1) *perturbed calibration* (nocodazole, MG132, STLC, MPS1 inhibition) to isolate specific mechanisms, knowing that nocodazole duration itself changes how much CENP-E arrives by alternative routes; (2) *unperturbed mitosis* (live-cell: time to congression, persistent polar chromosomes, anaphase errors per completed mitosis). An arrow between two layers is claimed only if measured.

### Legal 2020, figure-level notes (PDF with supplement and Table S1 supplied by the team)

- **Fig. 6 (key figure), inspected.** CENP-E/CENP-C per kinetochore with GFP-BUBR1 WT, the 1–1030 helix deletion, or no induction, after BUBR1 siRNA: (A–C) MG132 2.5 h; (D–F) plus a 5-min nocodazole pulse; (G–I) nocodazole 2.5 h. With the pulse, the deletion is as low as no induction; after 2.5 h the deletion stays lower than WT (the authors' own test marks the difference as significant), i.e. "much more similar", not equal.
- **Statistics.** n are kinetochores (about 130–230 per condition) from **two** independent experiments, tested with t-tests on kinetochores: replication is two experiments, not hundreds.
- **Caption vs figure.** Fig. 6K's axis reads "Mitotic cells with aligned chromosomes (%)" while the legend says "percentage of cells with at least one misaligned chromosome"; the values (WT about 55 %, deletion about 40 %, no induction about 28 %) only make sense as *aligned*. The legend also says kinetochores "at the metaphase plate" are plotted, including nocodazole conditions where no plate exists. Neither changes the direction of the result.
- **Expression.** Fig. 6L shows induced GFP-BUBR1 WT and 1–1030 (single-copy Flp-In clones) with degradation products; levels are described as similar to endogenous, judged from one blot.
- **Table S1 (CENP-E pull-down mass spectrometry).** BUBR1 has 7 spectral counts in the pull-down and 0 in the control (CENP-E bait 59; several abundant proteins have more): specific but modest enrichment; BUB3 (9) and MYPT1 (8) also appear.
- **Remaining Legal figures inspected (Figs. 1–5, S1–S3).** Fig. 4: BUBR1 705–1050 binds the CENP-E targeting domain (ITC Kd about 318 nM), 705–1030 does not, and the isolated 1031–1050 helix fused to MBP does not either: the helix is necessary, not sufficient. Fig. 4C aligns the BUBR1/BUB1 C-terminal segment; the case missense lies in that pseudokinase segment but outside the highlighted CENP-E-binding helix (sanitized). Fig. 5: a four-glutamate patch in CENP-E is essential. **S1D–E: kinetochore subpopulation matters** — the CENP-E targeting fragment is reduced at aligned kinetochores but not at polar ones, so "which kinetochores" must be scored, not only a cell mean. **S3: at attached kinetochores (MG132) and after a 5-min pulse, BUBR1 or BUB1 depletion lowers CENP-E while ZW10 (corona/RZZ) depletion does not**, consistent with the corona route being slow. S2B, the GST-only negative control for BUBR1 binding, is weak by design: GST and BUBR1 705–1050 elute at nearly the same volume, so co-elution and no binding look alike on SEC. Minor caption issues: Fig. 5 legend gives a reversed residue range; Fig. 1G misalignment counts appear capped at 10 per cell.
- **Status:** Legal 2020 main figures, supplement and Table S1 visually audited.

### Weber 2024, figure-level notes (article + supplement PDF supplied by the team)

- **Fig. 1, inspected.** CENP-E/CENP-C per kinetochore: (B) prometaphase vs metaphase vs dynein-heavy-chain siRNA; (D) 1 µM nocodazole 2 h with BUBR1, DHC or both siRNAs; (F) 3.3 µM nocodazole 15 h with BUBR1, ZW10 or both. **Effect sizes are modest on the figure**: medians shift only slightly (in F, BUBR1 depletion alone barely moves the median after 15 h), while very large n of kinetochores (about 250–1200 per condition, 3 experiments) make small shifts highly significant. This is consistent with Legal's time-dependence (after long arrest BUBR1 matters less), but the text's "strong reduction" must be read against the plotted medians.
- **Compartment is partly inferred.** The authors state they could not reproducibly see corona crescents in their cell line; corona targeting is shown with fragments, CDK1-inhibitor-induced detachable crescents and co-localisation with Spindly/Mad1 rather than in the unperturbed full-length setting.
- **Protocols differ between papers** (Legal: 3.3 µM 5-min pulse vs 0.3 µM 2.5 h; Weber: 1 µM 2 h and 3.3 µM 15 h). Numbers are not transferable across papers; A5/A6 must calibrate their own time points.
- **Figs. 2–4, inspected.** Fig. 2: the BUBR1-binding CENP-E fragment disappears from kinetochores after CDK1 inhibition (outer kinetochore disassembled) while the corona fragment stays; single-site loop mutants abolish corona targeting (n about 18–39 cells, experiments repeated twice). Fig. 3: in the *full-length* CENP-E (U2OS, 3.3 µM nocodazole) losing the BUBR1 site (E4A) or the corona loop (TS) each lowers CENP-E, and losing both nearly abolishes it: the two routes are **additive in a long arrest**. Fig. 4B: the **alignment readout is from over-expressed CENP-E fragments acting as dominant negatives**, not from BUBR1 variants; it says nothing directly about a BUBR1 truncation. Fig. 4G is an explicitly *proposed* model (fast BUBR1 route, slow corona route, dynein stripping).
- **What Weber does not test:** any BUBR1 mutant, any unperturbed-mitosis segregation readout. It informs the CENP-E side of A5 (which compartment, how fast), not the BUBR1 alleles.
- **Status:** Weber 2024 main figures visually audited; supplement Figs. S1–S4 legends read, images not yet inspected.


## Suijkerbuijk 2010 supplement (published PDF and supplement supplied by the team; all pages inspected)

Answers to the three questions set before freezing A5/A6:

1. **How much was the historical truncation over-expressed?** Main Fig. 2D quantifies plasmid-expressed truncation at about 2.1× wild type in U2OS and 3.7× in HeLa; the full blots (Suppl. Fig. S1) confirm a strong band. Its "full function" was therefore shown **above** wild-type level, while the unstable neighbour truncation was at 0.12–0.27×.
2. **Which segregation endpoint?** Main Figs. 4B and 5B: live imaging of H2B-EYFP U2OS after BUBR1 shRNA, fraction of anaphase cells with missegregating chromosomes, relative to BUBR1-depleted cells (≥238–268 cells, 3 experiments). This is an unperturbed-mitosis readout, but at over-expressed levels and with a coarse, cell-level score.
3. **What does the S5 model show?** Truncations act through absent transcript (NMD, marked with a question mark), kinase-domain substitutions through low abundance, one TPR substitution through checkpoint dysfunction; all converge on "BUBR1 hypomorph". The model already combines low abundance with direct functional impairment across alleles, but it does not claim both within one allele.

Further details found:
- **Construct identity (Suppl. Table 1):** the historical truncation is a splice-site variant producing a frameshift after about 713 native residues plus a short non-native tail. The case variant is a direct stop codon that would keep more native sequence and no frameshift tail. They are not interchangeable constructs; A5 must use the exact case product.
- **S3:** a shorter truncation delays mitosis (sustained checkpoint), from **one representative experiment**, ≥40 cells.
- **S2:** of two substitutions on one allele, only one is defective, showing that co-occurring variants must be tested separately.
